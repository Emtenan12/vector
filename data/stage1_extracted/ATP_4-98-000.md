### **Introduction** 

Army Sustainment Command (ASC) is a subordinate command of United States Army Materiel Command (USAMC). It integrates and synchronizes the delivery of USAMC materiel enterprise capabilities to supported Army Service component commands, corps, divisions, and installations through the employment of its subordinate Army field support brigades, Army field support battalions, and logistics readiness centers. ASC provides equipment and materiel support to operational Army units wherever required in support of the Army’s global power projection mission and assists with integrating Logistics Civil Augmentation Program capabilities into operations. 

This Army techniques publication provides an overview of ASC’s organizational structure and mission and describes how Army field support brigades, Army field support battalions, and logistics readiness centers provide access to USAMC’s broad range of strategic-level support to build and maintain combat power. 

ATP 4-98 contains four chapters and two appendices: 

**Chapter 1** provides an overview of USAMC and its subordinate lifecycle management commands. 

**Chapter 2** discusses ASC’s role, functions, and the programs it oversees. It further discusses ASC support to the Army Reserve and Army National Guard. 

**Chapter 3** provides an overview of Army field support brigade operations and describes how they integrate and synchronize USAMC capabilities in support of operations. 

**Chapter 4** provides an overview of Army field support battalion operations and describes how they integrate and synchronize USAMC capabilities in support of operations. 

**Appendix A** provides a framework for planning, developing, and publishing the Strategic Support Appendix. 

**Appendix B** provides information on logistics readiness centers.

##### **Chapter 1** 

### **Mission Overview** 

#### **ROLE OF UNITED STATES ARMY MATERIEL COMMAND** 

1-1. United States Army Materiel Command (USAMC) develops and delivers materiel readiness solutions to ensure globally dominant land force capabilities. Headquartered at Redstone Arsenal, Alabama, USAMC synchronizes and integrates the Army's total capabilities in support of the Chief of Staff of the Army’s priorities and combatant command requirements. As the Army’s lead materiel integrator, USAMC manages the global supply chain, synchronizing logistics and sustainment activities across the Army. See ATP 4-93 for additional information. 

1-2. USAMC plans and coordinates expeditionary force contracting support operations, providing contracting support for deployed units and installation-level services, supplies, minor military construction, the Army organic industrial base, and common-use information technology hardware and software. 

#### **USAMC LIFECYCLE MANAGEMENT COMMANDS** 

1-3. USAMC provides logistics, technology, acquisition support, and selected logistics support to Army forces as well as USAMC-related common support to other Services and mission partners. This is accomplished principally through national-level maintenance and supply programs managed and executed by the life cycle management commands (LCMCs) placed under the operational control (OPCON) of Army field support brigades (AFSBs) when deployed. LCMCs are the USAMC organizations that have the responsibility to mobilize and deploy sustainment-level maintenance forward repair activities (FRAs) from continental United States (CONUS) LCMC depots and arsenals. LCMCs conduct theater-level contingency planning with USAMC, Army Sustainment Command (ASC), and theater sustainment commands (TSCs) during set-the-theater deliberate planning. These LCMCs include USAMC staff as well as related Assistant Secretary of the Army for Acquisition, Logistics, and Technology (ASA[ALT]) program executive and program management offices. These LCMC and ASA(ALT) elements work together to ensure support for fielded weapon systems and equipment for their entire life cycle. Program executive office (PEO) and program management office (PMO) staffs often work in the same office or on the ground in the field along with LCMC item managers and other technical support personnel. The LCMCs are— 

- United States Army Communications-Electronics Command (CECOM). This command develops, acquires, fields, and sustains Army communications systems. It provides significant technical support capabilities to deploying and deployed Army forces. 

- United States Army Tank-Automotive and Armaments Command (TACOM). This command develops, acquires, fields, and sustains Soldier and ground systems for the operational Army through the integration of effective and timely acquisition, logistics, and cutting-edge technology. 

- United States Aviation and Missile Command (AMCOM). This command develops, acquires, fields, and sustains aviation, missile, and unmanned vehicle systems, ensuring system readiness with seamless transition to operations. It transitions science and technology into aviation, missile, and unmanned vehicle systems and provides calibration materiel readiness. 

- The Joint Munitions and Lethality Command. This command develops, acquires, fields, and sustains ammunition for the joint force. Core competencies include: research, development, and engineering acquisition and program management; logistics management; industrial operations; contracting; serving as the single manager for conventional ammunition executor and field operating activity; performing demilitarization and disposal of unserviceable stocks; conducting industrial base management and executing transformation; providing real-time munitions readiness reporting; maintaining worldwide asset visibility; centrally managing ammunition; and providing integrated lethality solutions. 

- United States Army Medical Logistics Command. This command is the LCMC for medical materiel. It executes the medical materiel management functions for combatant commands to set and sustain operational medical capabilities in all phases of large-scale combat operations. Core capabilities include: medical materiel storage and distribution; supply chain management and integration;

##### **Chapter 2** 

### **Army Sustainment Command** 

#### **ROLE OF ARMY SUSTAINMENT COMMAND** 

2-1. ASC is a major subordinate command of USAMC. ASC integrates and synchronizes the delivery of USAMC and materiel enterprise capabilities at echelon from the strategic to the tactical level. It delivers materiel readiness, force generation, power projection, and sets the conditions for future readiness at home station. ASC forward stationed capabilities provide command and control to USAMC/ASC assets in theater and shape the logistics environment and help set the theater to accelerate force reception. Deployable logistics support elements (LSEs) provide corps and divisions the ability to rapidly integrate the delivery of USAMC capabilities into the theater at echelon for responsive support to warfighter priorities. ASC serves as the Army’s lead materiel integrator, identifying excess and obsolete major end items and providing disposition instructions to units. It is responsible for integrating logistics support with strategic partners and links the national sustainment base with the expeditionary Army. Major mission areas include logistics synchronization in support of the Army Regionally Aligned Readiness and Modernization Model, Army prepositioned stocks (APS), materiel management, and the Logistics Civil Augmentation Program (LOGCAP). ASC executes its LOGCAP mission through a global network of organizations to include the ASC staff, AFSBs, Army field support battalions (AFSBns), logistics readiness centers (LRCs), and LSEs embedded in units at the division and corps. See FM 4-0 for additional information on ASC. 

#### **SUPPORT OPERATIONS DIRECTORATE** 

2-2. The Support Operations Directorate executes program management for APS, LRCs, the Logistics Assistance Program (LAP), and second destination transportation and Joint Personal Property Shipping Office. It plans and directs sustainment operations and integrates and synchronizes readiness enablers including the Army Oil Analysis Program, Production Support Division, Army Airlift Clearance Authority, Army Petroleum Center, and the Packaging, Storage, and Containerization Center. It also conducts distribution and redistribution of Class VII and Class II durable items as the Army’s lead materiel integrator to enable the readiness of the Army, the Army’s power projection capabilities, and defense support to civil authorities. Additional Support Operations Directorate missions include— 

- Support to exercises. 

- Installation-level baseline logistic services. 

- APS modernization. 

- Model supply support activity (SSA). 

- Central issuing facility storefront. 

- Warrior Restaurant Program. 

- Installation SSA reform. 

- Army Oil Analysis Program. 

- Authorized stockage list and integrated shop stock list development, implementation, and sustainment. 

- Stock readiness compliance inspections. 

- Class III(B) requirements validation and coordination with Defense Logistics Agency. 

- Cadet summer training. 

- Mobilization force generation installation (MFGI)/power projection platform. 

#### **LEAD MATERIEL INTEGRATOR** 

2-3. As USAMC’s lead materiel integrator, ASC provides materiel readiness visibility and management, including property accountability and source of repair work loading. ASC’s support operations (SPO) section executes materiel management and materiel distribution in support of the Army by coordinating the distribution and redistribution of Army equipment and intensively managed items in accordance with priorities and policies established by Headquarters, Department of the Army (HQDA). It also synchronizes the execution of SSA reorganization or closure and equipping requirements for the global components:

#### **LOGISTICS ASSISTANCE PROGRAM** 

2-4. The LAP is a HQDA program executed by ASC that aids in the early detection and resolution of logistics-related problems affecting materiel readiness. The LAP provides commanders with the technical experience and guidance necessary to resolve weapon system, equipment, and systemic logistics problems. LAP representatives conduct logistics assessments to determine unit status and analyze historical trends to provide corrective and preventative measures for improving unit readiness. The program also provides equipment technical support and augmentative maintainer training to units and Soldiers. See AR 700-4 for further information on the LAP. 

#### **INSTALLATION LOGISTICS SUPPORT** 

2-5. ASC serves as a central sustainment enabler delivering installation logistics support that builds and maintains installation readiness. ASC’s Transportation Readiness Directorate enables ASC and USAMC to accomplish strategic initiatives in installation readiness, strategic power projection, MFGI activities, and unit deployment readiness. It integrates and synchronizes USAMC transportation capabilities at the operational and tactical level, providing Installation Logistics Baseline Standards, unit deployment support, and execution of the Army’s Second Destination Transportation Program. It is comprised of three divisions: the Second Destination Transportation Division, the Transportation Services Division, and the Mobility Division. 

2-6. The Second Destination Transportation Division has four primary responsibilities: third-party payment system administration, transportation accounting code administration and management, centrally managed accounts, and operational mission transportation accounting code execution and management. It also conducts non-operational missions focused on Army post office mail, subsistence, transportation operations, and the Army and Air Force Exchange Service. 

2-7. The Transportation Services Directorate maintains liaison and provides comprehensive guidance and transportation management policy, program guidance, and oversight to ASC personal property offices. It provides Army process support and guidance to United States Army Central, USAMC, and Department of Defense (DOD) joint Service personal property offices as required. It coordinates the Defense Personal Property Program (also called DP3) business rules with United States Transportation Command, Defense Finance and Accounting Service, and joint Service headquarters. It coordinates response, vetting, and routing for all Army joint travel regulation personal property exceptions to policy with HQDA for all Soldier and civilian entitlement issues received. It provides updates to HQDA Personally Procured Move and Army Mobilization and Deployment Reference required policy updates. The Transportation Services Directorate provides inventory management and acquisitions oversight for General Services Administration (GSA)leased non-tactical vehicles between ASC subordinate organizations in accordance with Installation Logistics Baseline Standards and ensures vehicle requirements remain within regulatory guidance. 

- **Personal Property/Household goods shipments:** The Transportation Services Division maintains liaison and provides comprehensive guidance and transportation management policy, program guidance and oversight to ASC personal property offices. It provides command oversight for Army personal property support manpower structure and functional requirements. It provides Army process support and guidance to USAMC and DOD joint Service personal property offices as required and coordinates Defense Personal Property Program business rules with United States Transportation Command, Defense Finance and Accounting Service, and the joint Service headquarters. 

- **GSA non-tactical vehicle management:** The Transportation Services Division provides inventory management and acquisitions oversight for GSA-leased non-tactical vehicles between ASC subordinate organizations, ASC headquarters, USAMC, HQDA, and multiple Army commands. The division ensures support meets customer GSA-leased vehicle requirements and remains within regulatory guidance.

#### **ARMY PREPOSITIONED STOCKS** 

2-9. HQDA owns APS, which are managed by USAMC, Office of the Surgeon General, and the Defense Logistics Agency. These managing activities maintain, account for, and care for APS in storage worldwide. USAMC executes APS management through ASC. APS stocks include combat equipment, supplies, and humanitarian mission stocks located around the globe to support rapid power projection to contingency areas. APS reduces the initial amount of strategic lift required to support a predominately CONUS-based Army and sustain the deployed force until sea lines of communication are established. The release authority for APS resides with the Secretary of Defense, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, Chief of Staff of the Army, and HQDA G-3/5, and is coordinated by the requesting command through HQDA. When APS is authorized for release, the ASC SPO coordinates through the AFSB for the delivery and issue of major end items and limited secondary items from the APS program to the designated operational area when required by the theater Army. The designated AFSB assists in calling forward APS equipment before releasing the items to gaining units. At the port of debarkation or hand-off area, teams from the designated AFSBn transfer the equipment to the gaining unit with support from the theater opening elements. 

2-10. The three categories of APS are: 

- **Unit sets** include end items and support materiel configured for combat (to include authorized stockage list, shop stock, and unit basic load) that are positioned ashore and afloat to reduce deployment response time and support the Army’s force projection strategy. 

- **Operational project stocks** are comprised of materiel above normal modified table of organization and equipment, table of distribution and allowances (TDA), and common table of allowance authorizations. Operational project stocks are tailored to key strategic capabilities essential to the Army’s ability to execute its force projection strategy and are designed to support one or more Army operations, plans, or contingencies. 

- **War reserve stocks for allies** are directed by the Office of the Secretary of Defense to ensure the U.S. is prepared to assist designated allies in case of war. The U.S. owns and finances war reserve stocks for allies, and they are pre-positioned in designated forward locations. These stocks are released to the proper theater Army for transfer to the supported mission partners under provisions in the Foreign Assistance Act and under existing country-to-country memorandums of agreement.

#### **ADDITIONAL ASC PROGRAMS AND CAPABILITIES** 

2-12. ASC manages several additional programs and capabilities with expertise available through coordination with the AFSB: 

- The Army Oil Analysis Program enhances combat readiness by extending the life of oil-wetted components, detecting impending failures early before catastrophic failure occurs. 

- Stockage Determination Division, formally known as the Army expert authorized stockage list, generates and manages Army authorized stockage list stockage objectives worldwide. It is the sole source for authorized stockage list and integrated shop stock list development, implementation, and sustainment products worldwide, and it supports the Army supply system in an enterprise resource planning environment.

- The Production Support Division provides sustainment support and manages all Army DOD activity address codes, routing identifier codes, property book identification, and modifies and maintains the unit identification codes (UICs) and defense activity address codes in the Army Enterprise System Integration Program. 

#### **ARMY RESERVE AND ARMY NATIONAL GUARD SUPPORT** 

2-13. ASC partners with Army Reserve and Army National Guard units in support of the Army’s multicomponent sustainment solution. Reserve sustainment units integrate with ASC equities for training in support of large-scale mobilization and combat operations. Training opportunities include support to warfighter exercises, combat training center rotations, MFGI mobilization exercises, and other training on the HQDA Exercise Priority List. 

2-14. These Reserve sustainment units can deploy an LSE in large-scale combat operations to provide planning, integration, and reach-back capabilities of USAMC and materiel enterprise capabilities. Reserve LSEs can be utilized both within and outside of CONUS in accordance with ASC priorities.

##### **Chapter 3** 

### **Army Field Support Brigades** 

#### **MISSION OVERVIEW** 

3-1. The AFSB’s mission is to integrate and synchronize USAMC strategic capabilities (APS, LAP technical support, LRCs, strategic-level materiel management, sustainment maintenance, and LOGCAP) in support of ASCCs and corps. The AFSB and its subordinate battalions provide access to USAMC’s broad range of strategic-level support to build and maintain combat power. The AFSB provides this support to the operational and tactical echelons of command in support of operations. The AFSB meets its mission requirements through its assigned and attached subordinate organizations in CONUS, or through theater AFSBs, USAMC reach-back/call forward capabilities, and contracted support outside of CONUS. Specific AFSB mission responsibilities include— 

- Maintaining accountability of specified Army contractors authorized to accompany the force and other theater-designated contractor personnel in coordination with the supported units and contracting support brigade. 

- Coordinating support from the national sustainment base, including expert advice and call forward assistance regarding readiness and sustainment. 

- Integrating and synchronizing LOGCAP planning for the combatant command and ASCC staffs to manage cost, schedule, and performance tradeoffs and mitigate risk. 

- Integrating and synchronizing LOGCAP and Enhanced Army Global Logistics Enterprise support at the strategic, operational, and tactical level. 

- Managing APS to include off-loading and property accountability. 

- Managing the LAP through attached AFSBns and other USAMC logistics organizations called forward. 

- Commanding sustainment maintenance organizations deployed to the theater. These organizations include FRAs, combat vehicle evaluation teams, and equipment support activities. 

- Identifying, storing, and coordinating the distribution/redistribution of designated enduring and excess non-theater-provided Class VII equipment in accordance with theater policies and procedures. 

- Integrating (in coordination with the contracting support brigade commander) the ASCC-developed Annex W (operational contract support) into the overall AFSB support plan. The annex W appendices of 1) Contracting Capabilities and Capacities Support Estimate; 2) Contractor Management Plan; and 3) Summary of Contractor Support Estimate are key to ensuring operational contract support from requirements and contracting support to contractor management. 

- Providing contractor supplemental maintenance capability for tactical unit equipment in an area of responsibility with maintenance support teams in forward areas. 

- Providing ASCC, joint, and combined services with reimbursable contracted maintenance support. 

- Synchronizing USAMC capabilities in support of defense support of civil authorities and humanitarian assistance and disaster relief. 

- Requesting Joint Munitions Command quality assurance specialist (ammunition surveillance) support in coordination with ASCCs and TSCs. 

- AFSBs will coordinate with materiel developers after a materiel fielding agreement is in place to establish outside CONUS staging sites. Refer to AR 770-2 for additional details. When supporting a field army, the AFSB and its subordinate battalions and call forward capabilities are dependent upon the support elements within the operational area for protection, health service support, signal support, religious support, legal support, financial management support, administrative support, and logistics services.

##### **COMMAND SECTION** 

3-6. The AFSB command section includes a commander, command sergeant major, and executive officer. The commander provides mission command to the assigned AFSBns and attached USAMC organizations. The AFSB commander serves as the lead USAMC/ASC sustainment maintenance authority and senior USAMC advisor within the operational area. The office of the commander is normally comprised of a DA Civilian deputy to the commander, a Command Sergeant Major, and management support analyst. 

3-7. The DA Civilian deputy to the commander serves as the commander’s principal assistant in planning, coordinating, and directing operations. The deputy to the commander assists in the supervision and oversight of the brigade staff and geographically dispersed subordinate organizations. The deputy also assists in command oversight of external mission support, LAP, and synchronization of acquisition, logistics, and technology. 

3-8. The executive officer provides supervision and oversight of the brigade staff and can deploy as the LSE director during contingency operations. The executive officer also serves as the corps advisor in the absence of the commander. 

##### **STAFF SECTIONS** 

3-9. The coordinating staff of the AFSB includes the S-1 through S-8, safety operations, and SPO. The AFSB coordinating staff conducts current and future operations planning and coordination of AFSB external support. 

3-10. The SPO office is responsible for all matters concerning AFSB external support operations. It is responsible for overseeing LAP operations, materiel management, sustainment-level maintenance, supply, transportation, ammunition, food service, contract management, and GCSS-Army operations for supported units. #### **MISSION OVERVIEW** 

4-1. AFSBns are scalable and tailored TDA organizations. The direct support-readiness AFSBns integrate and synchronize materiel enterprise capabilities in direct support to divisions and tenant units and the installation area support missions identified in AR 525-93 and DA Pam 525-93. Theater AFSBns provide general support to units within their designated support area. APS AFSBns provide the physical management of Army pre-positioned stocks. 

4-2. AFSBns conduct three types of missions: provide readiness support, provide technical expertise, and provide maintenance. Designated AFSBns have habitual support relationships with their supported divisions and serve as USAMC’s lead materiel integrator for the division and tenant units. The AFSBn utilizes LARs to provide equipment technical expertise to assist in identifying and resolving systemic maintenance problems to build and maintain combat power. ##### **Appendix A** 

### **Strategic Support** 

A-1. This appendix is a framework for planning, developing, and publishing the Strategic Support Appendix to Annex F (Sustainment) of the operation order or operation plan. AFSB commanders and planners will work with planners at their supported units to ensure integration of USAMC initiatives and capabilities in the operations planning and orders processes. 

#### **1. TASK ORGANIZATION** 

USAMC delivers national strategic and theater strategic enterprise capabilities organized under its major subordinate commands: ASC, Installation Management Command, Financial Management Command, Military Surface Deployment and Distribution Command, Security Assistance Command, Joint Munitions Command and Joint Munitions and Lethality Command, Army Contracting Command, CECOM, Army Medical Logistics Command, AMCOM, and TACOM. 

#### **2. SITUATION** 

USAMC conducts globally integrated logistics operations in a complicated, interconnected, transregional environment. These operations involve the total force, which consists of the Active-Duty units, Reserve units, DA Civilians, and contracted support. Globalization, technology advancements, antiaccess/area denial, and flexible threats create a complex, ever-changing operational environment. The essential challenge is to support unified action by meeting increasingly demanding logistics requirements with constrained resources in a potentially contested environment. 

#### **3. EXECUTION** 

##### **A. MISSION** 

USAMC delivers precision sustainment and materiel readiness to an expeditionary global force from the joint strategic support area to the tactical point of need across the spectrum of conflict in support of the joint force, employing ASC as the chief integrator that synchronizes key elements of the sustainment enterprise to deliver capabilities in support of Army forces during joint operations. 

##### **B. SCHEME OF SUPPORT** 

USAMC delivers key national strategic and theater strategic sustainment enterprise capabilities within the framework of its core logistics functions: deployment and distribution, supply, maintenance, logistics services, contract support, and medical logistics. ASC integrates and synchronizes key elements of the sustainment enterprise to deliver ready forces, enable strategic power projection, and sustain operational reach to win during operations. ASC’s focus is Army-specific support, but support to joint and mission partners is often required based on cross-servicing agreements. ASC accomplishes this mission through its subordinate units and collaboration with higher and lateral commands. 

##### **C. TASKS TO UNITS**

ASC integrates and synchronizes the delivery of USAMC key national strategic and theater strategic enterprise capabilities through a global network of the ASC staff, AFSBs, AFSBns, LRCs, and LSEs.

Installation Management Command delivers quality base support from the strategic support area enabling readiness for a globally responsive Army.

#### **4. SUSTAINMENT** 

##### **A. FORCE PROJECTION**

##### **C. ARMY PREPOSITIONED STOCKS** 

The APS program constitutes one of the three legs of the Strategic Mobility Triad. The purposes of APS are twofold: to reduce the initial amount of strategic lift required to support CONUS-based power projection and to sustain the warfighter until sea lines of communication with CONUS are established and industrial base surge capacity is achieved. APS is owned by DA and is approved for release by Deputy Chief of Staff, G-3/5/7. APS is designed and resourced for Army use. Assets may be repositioned between or among theaters at the direction of HQDA depending on the current situation. APS consists of protected go-to-war assets. These stocks may not be used for purposes such as improving peacetime readiness, as maintenance floats, or for filling unit shortages. 

###### **1. APS Validation** 

The HQDA G-3 validates equipment requirements and establishes authorizations for unit sets and operational project stocks for prepositioning to satisfy ASCC operation plan requirements according to processes codified in AR 710-1. 

###### **2. APS Management** 

ASC, as a USAMC subordinate, manages APS by accounting for and maintaining equipment according to AR 750-1, AR 770-2, TM 38-470, and applicable -10/-20 technical manuals. ASC executes this mission through AFSBs and their subordinate AFSBns. AFSBns prepare all APS equipment for storage, provide COSIS, and issue from storage.
